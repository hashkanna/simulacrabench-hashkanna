\begin{abstract}
  We forecast each blank PREDICT cell by its conditional marginal given the GIVEN block, which
  is what the benchmark's per-cell log score rewards. Everything is fitted at run time, inside
  \texttt{predict()}, on the visible respondents. Five experts produce out-of-fold (OOF)
  forecasts: smoothed marginals, multi-task logistic regression, a multi-task MLP, the frozen
  tabular foundation model TabPFN-3.5 used in context with up to eight ensemble members, and a
  gate-decomposition model for items whose skip-logic parent is itself predicted. Per item, they
  are combined by non-negative log-linear pooling fitted to the grader's floored loss; ordinal
  smoothing, skip-logic gates and structural zeros are applied only where they do not raise OOF
  loss. A wall-clock planner sizes the TabPFN ensemble so that every item is covered within the
  time limit. Components were chosen on proxy instruments built from public surveys. The
  submission's test-phase skill on the leaderboard is \TestSkill{} (UNICEF \TestUNICEF, World
  Bank \TestWB, UNHCR \TestUNHCR).
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
For each instrument, \texttt{predict()} receives the visible respondents with all columns and the
held-out respondents with the GIVEN block only. It returns a distribution over the options of
every blank PREDICT cell. Each cell is scored on its own, so the optimal forecast under the log
score~\citep{gneiting2007strictly} is the conditional marginal $P(y_j \mid x)$ of item $j$ given
the GIVEN vector $x$. Joint structure between items, such as skip logic and recording rules,
matters only where it helps to estimate these marginals. At run time, on the visible rows only,
the pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item integer-codes the GIVEN and PREDICT columns and splits the visible rows into five folds;
  \item gets each expert's OOF (visible rows) and held-out forecasts (Section~\ref{sec:model});
  \item adds a gate-decomposition forecast for items whose skip-logic parent is a PREDICT item,
        built from the parent's pooled forecast (parents are processed first, so chains compose);
  \item pools the experts per item (Section~\ref{sec:pool}); and
  \item post-processes each item where that does not raise its OOF loss
        (Section~\ref{sec:post}).
\end{enumerate}
This is stacking~\citep{wolpert1992stacked} with a log-linear (geometric) combination
rule~\citep{genest1986combining,hinton2002training}. The strongest expert is the prior-data
fitted network TabPFN~\citep{hollmann2023tabpfn,hollmann2025tabpfn}, in its 3.5
release~\citep{priorlabs2026tabpfn35}.

\subsection{Inputs and feature construction}
Only the GIVEN block is used: 7, 9 and 17 columns for UNICEF, World Bank and UNHCR
(for UNHCR: survey round, country of asylum, governorate, governorate of origin, relationship to
the household head, age band, education, marital status, single-parent status and eight
household-composition count bands). Logistic regression and the MLP use one-hot codes. TabPFN
receives integer codes, with non-numeric columns declared categorical and numeric bands kept in
order, plus one numeric feature per group of count columns that share a question: the sum of
their parsed values. Respondent identifiers, question text and survey weights are not used; the
schema supplies option lists, gates and the numeric values of banded options. No
synthetic units are constructed.

\subsection{Model or simulator}
\label{sec:model}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,label=(\roman*)]
  \item \emph{Marginal}: visible counts plus $\nicefrac12$, the benchmark's reference forecast.
  \item \emph{Logistic regression}: a multinomial softmax per item on one-hot GIVEN, all items
        fitted jointly by L-BFGS~\citep{liu1989limited}; ridge penalty per item from
        $\{1,4,16,64\}$ by OOF loss.
  \item \emph{MLP}: a shared trunk of two 128-unit GELU layers with dropout 0.25, a linear skip
        path and per-item softmax heads, trained with AdamW~\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}. Two
        seeds are averaged.
  \item \emph{TabPFN-3.5}, frozen and used in context: per item, the classifier is conditioned
        on labelled visible rows and queried on the target rows, with up to eight ensemble
        members.
  \item \emph{Gate decomposition}, for a child $c$ of a PREDICT parent $\pi$: a multi-task
        logistic model of $P(c \mid x, \pi{=}v)$ on one-hot $x$, one-hot $v$ and $v \times$
        GIVEN interactions for GIVEN columns with at most four levels (these include the survey
        round, so round-specific recording rules can be learned), marginalised over the parent's
        pooled forecast: $\sum_v \hat P(\pi{=}v \mid x)\,P(c \mid x, v)$.
\end{enumerate}
Cells are forecast independently given $x$, which is exact for a per-cell score.

\subsection{Fitting, training, or prompting}
All fitting happens at run time on the visible rows of the current phase (TRAIN and DEV in the
test phase); nothing is trained offline on SimulacraBench data, and TabPFN's weights are never
updated. The logistic models minimise ridge-penalised multinomial log loss. The MLP's number of
epochs (at most 60) is chosen on an inner validation split (its other settings are in
Appendix~\ref{app:config}). Ridge strengths, pooling weights and post-processing switches are
chosen per item on five-fold OOF loss. In the test phase TabPFN's OOF forecasts come from five
folds over all visible rows, each conditioned on the other 80\%; the 15-minute development phase
used two folds over a calibration subset.

Which experts to run, the ensemble size and the feature encoding were chosen offline on proxy
instruments (Section~\ref{sec:resources}), comparing configurations by a paired bootstrap
clustered by respondent. On the UNHCR-like proxy the in-context experts added 0.023 skill to the
classical ones, and no pool that added TabICL~\citep{qu2025tabicl} or TabPFN-3.5-Fast to
eight-member TabPFN-3.5 beat TabPFN-3.5 alone (Appendix~\ref{app:results}). The development
leaderboard only confirmed that runs completed.

\subsection{Prediction and aggregation}
\label{sec:pool}
For item $j$ with experts $m = 1, \dots, M$, the pooled forecast is
$p_j(y \mid x) \propto \prod_m p_{jm}(y \mid x)^{w_{jm}}$ with $0 \le w_{jm} \le 5$. The weights
minimise the OOF loss under the grader's floor,
$-\frac1n \sum_i \log\big(10^{-3} + (1 - K_j \cdot 10^{-3})\, p_j(y_i \mid x_i)\big)$
for an item with $K_j$ options. A ridge penalty $\frac{2}{\sqrt n}\lVert w_j - \bar w\rVert^2$
pulls them toward $\bar w$, the median of unpenalised weights across items. They are fitted by
L-BFGS-B. The weights need not sum to one, so their sum acts as a temperature. Held-out
forecasts use the same weights. No survey weights are used.

\subsection{Post-processing and calibration (if applicable)}
\label{sec:post}
Three steps follow, in this order, each kept per item only if it does not raise OOF loss.
(i)~\emph{Ordinal smoothing}: for numerically banded options the forecast is blended with its
three-point moving average, with a weight from $\{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5\}$.
(ii)~\emph{Gates with a GIVEN parent}: the released gates are not always deterministic, so the
probability that the item is asked given parent value $v$ is estimated as
$(a_v + \tfrac12 + \tfrac12 s_v)/(n_v + \tfrac32)$, where $a_v$ of the $n_v$ visible rows with
that value were asked and $s_v = 1$ if the schema marks $v$ as asked; the forecast's answer mass
is rescaled to it.
(iii)~\emph{Structural zeros}: an option never observed among the $n \ge 60$ visible rows that
share a GIVEN value is capped at $0.5/(n+1)$, and the excess is redistributed.
TabPFN's outputs are mixed with 0.1\% of the smoothed marginal so that options absent from a
context keep some mass.

\subsection{Breakdown by population, survey, or question type}
By instrument, the method does best on UNHCR, whose items (return intentions, needs, safety,
services) are mostly gated: who is asked and what they answer are largely predictable from the
GIVEN columns. It does worst on World Bank, whose psychometric
scales, cognitive-task scores and programme feedback are only weakly determined by the
demographic and labour-market GIVEN columns. UNICEF (attitudes and behaviours on COVID-19 and
routine vaccination) lies in between.

        measured speed, so a much slower GPU can give slightly different forecasts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Components were chosen on proxy surveys that differ from the targets in population and
questionnaire, so their effects transfer only approximately. The method yields per-cell
marginals, not coherent synthetic respondents, and its strongest expert is a single pretrained
model under a non-commercial licence. The UNHCR file pools two survey rounds without a
cross-round key, so a held-out household may resemble a visible one; we perform no record
matching, but TabPFN's attention can use such resemblances, and UNHCR scores may therefore
overstate accuracy on households not seen before.\label{endmain}

\paragraph{Choosing the in-context experts.} These comparisons use cached forecasts on four
proxy splits. Full TabPFN-3.5 in place of TabPFN-3.5-Fast (one member each, with TabICL) gave
$+0.0023$ and $+0.0016$ on the two REACH splits (2--4 SE) and no change elsewhere. For full
TabPFN-3.5, four members instead of one gave $+0.0007$ to $+0.0018$ on REACH, and eight instead of
four a further $+0.0002$ on average, positive on all four proxies; for Fast and TabICL, four
members added nothing. With eight-member TabPFN-3.5 in the pool, adding TabICL, Fast or the
multiclass TabPFN-3.5 variant changed skill by $-0.0002$ to $+0.0000$. The pooling ridge works
best at its default strength: ten and a hundred times stronger cost $0.0002$ and $0.0007$, and
fully shared weights $0.0012$.

\paragraph{Other effects.} Gate decomposition on top of the in-context experts: $+0.0013$ and
$+0.0004$ on REACH (SE $\le 0.0004$), no change on WGM. The MLP on top of TabPFN: $+0.0005$ on all
three proxies. Target-ordered encoding of nominal columns for TabPFN: $-0.001$ to $-0.002$, so it
is not used.

\paragraph{Repeated households.} Because the UNHCR file pools two survey rounds, some held-out
households may have a near-copy among the visible rows. We asked whether TabICL, reported to use
such copies well, would add to TabPFN-3.5 when they exist. In a variant of the REACH proxy, 64\%
of respondents receive a copy: each PREDICT answer is kept with probability 0.8 (otherwise
replaced by another respondent's), and each GIVEN value is resampled with probability 0.05. The
copies raise skill from 0.484 to 0.532. Adding TabICL (four members) to TabPFN-3.5 (eight members)
changed skill by $-0.0002$ (SE 0.0002): $+0.0005$ (SE 0.0003) on rows whose copy was visible and
$-0.0016$ (SE 0.0004) on the others. TabICL alone scored 0.019 lower. With exact copies (all GIVEN
values kept, 95\% of answers kept) skill rose to 0.725; adding TabICL changed it by $-0.00002$
(SE 0.00009), and TabICL alone scored 0.021 lower. TabPFN-3.5 alone was therefore kept. No record
matching is performed; copies can matter only through TabPFN's attention over the visible rows.

\paragraph{Timing.} The timings are of an earlier build with the same expert pool, run on the
kit's sandbox data on a rented H100 (8 CPU cores, 16\,GB RAM, Python 3.13). In the test-phase plan
eight-member TabPFN-3.5 took 9.2, 3.0 and 5.2\,s per item on UNICEF, World Bank and UNHCR, and the
three instruments took 142, 268 and 1{,}151\,s (1{,}562\,s in all, of 3{,}600\,s); the development
configuration took 475\,s of its 900\,s. The submitted build was not timed again on an H100; it
completed on the platform within both limits.
